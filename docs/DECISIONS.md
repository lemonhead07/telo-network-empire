# Locked Decisions

These settle the ten conflicts in `TELO_GAMEPLAY_HANDOFF.md` §7. Change one only on purpose, and record why.

| # | Question | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Which codebase? | **New single codebase: Godot 4.7 .NET (C#)**, pure sim in `src/Telo.Sim`. The old Unity/web repo (`~/oldNetworkNez`) is reference only. Port logic from it into `Telo.Sim` when useful. | Already installed; no royalties or runtime fees; C# lets the sim be tested with plain `dotnet test`; exports to Windows, Linux and Steam Deck. |
| D2 | Start year | **3 Jan 1983.** Act 0 is short (the first 1–2 hours) and teaches the basics. | Matches canon. Starting small is the point. |
| D3 | Perspective | **First-person** for physical work. Strategy views (notebook, PIP, dashboards) are objects you open. | Canon pillar 1; matches the hands-on job-sim genre. |
| D4 | Tech gating | **Announcement queue + act milestones.** The calendar only matters through announcement dates the player can shift, **or skip: see D17.** | Canon. Implemented in `ActRules` / `AlmanacRules`. |
| D5 | Archive pacing | 0.2/day base + 0.05 per subscriber + 0.3 per staff member. Entries cost 8–30 (100 for Exchange 0). Tune in playtests. | With about 10 subscribers you recall roughly one entry every 2–3 in-game weeks. |
| D6 | Outages | Defects are **latent and surface later** (built). A diagnosis minigame comes in milestone M3. | Fits "chaos persists". |
| D7 | Installations | **Physical, at the frame** (built). Delegated once you've hired someone. | The core fantasy. |
| D8 | Scope | **Early Access = 1983–1995 (Acts 0–2)** on the whole planet: the full world is explorable from day one, but your network only reaches the first continent. Target 15–25 hours. 1.0 adds 1995–2007 (boom, broadband, undersea cables to other continents). Later updates add mobile, platform, and the satellite/space era. | Ship something people can buy; each later era gives the planet a new use. |
| D9 | Test truth | `dotnet test Telo.slnx` + `--smoke` are the measure of a *release* being sound. Day to day, see D16. | One number, reproducible. |
| D10 | Time | One in-game day = 8 real minutes (a full 24-hour sun cycle). The bed skips to morning. Days pause while menus are open. | Long enough to drive somewhere and back. Tune with `GameRoot.DayLengthSeconds`. |
| D11 | Monetisation | **Premium price, no in-game ads, no microtransactions.** See `BUSINESS_PLAN.md`. | Steam players punish ads in paid games. |
| D12 | World | **One small open-world planet, 2.5 km radius.** A lap is ~16 km: about 5 minutes by light aircraft and 12–20 minutes by van once roads wind around terrain. ~78 km² of surface for towns, roads, towers and cables. Terrain is a pure function in `Telo.Sim/Planet` (the sim can reason about distance, coverage and line-of-sight); Godot only draws it. | The curve of the world is the telecom game: horizons limit radio, oceans need cables, and the last era launches satellites. |
| D13 | Planet seed | **One fixed world (seed 1983)** for now, so towns and content can be hand-placed on known terrain. `--seed=N` exists for experiments. | A designed world beats a random one for a story game. |
| D14 | Travel | Walking is for the last few metres. Steep ground stops you on foot (46°) and in the van (36°); the sea stops the van. Roads, bridges, boats and aircraft are *earned* ways past terrain. | Terrain is the consistent, simple challenge. |
| D15 | Assets | **Models and textures are made in Blender, from Python scripts, and imported as `.glb`** (`tools/*.py` → `game/assets/`), so every asset can be regenerated and adjusted from code. Small painted or generated textures are allowed. The code-built `LowPoly` boxes remain the way to *prototype*: every new thing ships first as a labelled box and is replaced by a model later. Sound is still synthesised in code. What to make, and in what order: `ASSET_LIST.md`. **(Changed 2026-11-12 by the owner; it used to say "everything is made in code, no imported art". See D21.)** | The owner wants real models, textures and characters, and a trailer. Scripted Blender keeps the solo, reproducible workflow. |
| D16 | Testing | **Develop lots, test little.** Tests and the smoke run are *not* run after every change — only before a commit that changes game rules, before a release, or when asked. GitHub Actions is manual-trigger only. | The owner's preference; keeps iteration fast and CI free. |
| D17 | Starting an act early | **Recalling the act's key page from PIP's Almanac starts the act now**, at a cost in Archive and Echo. Waiting for the public date stays free. Starting early **puts you ahead**: the head start is recorded (`GameState.HeadStartDays`), and rival companies, when they exist, start from the public date. | Owner, 2026-11-12: "unlocking the stage puts you ahead; eventually there will be other companies to compete with." Removes the 16-month wait for Act 1 and makes the Almanac matter in hour one. |
| D18 | What a line is for | **Subscribers ring the Board** (a dial-in bulletin board in your room). It has a limited number of lines in; the evening rush causes engaged tones. | Gives every line a purpose and gives the game its first bottleneck. Canon Act 1. |
| D19 | Next place | **Saltmarsh Quay** (fishing village) before Harrow Ridge (radio link). | Reuses pole lines and installs: mostly content, little new machinery. |
| D20 | Poles | **The player surveys the route from Round 2 on.** The farm line keeps fixed pole sites as the tutorial. | Choosing the route is where terrain, permission and cost become decisions. |
| D21 | Imported models | **Allowed now.** See D15. | The owner's wish; `ASSET_LIST.md` assumes it. |
| D22 | Things customers can buy | **A catalogue of products** (`game/content/products.json`): extensions, answering machines, faster modems, second lines, fax machines and more. Existing subscribers order them; each is a short hands-on job with its own steps and kit. New technology from the Almanac adds new things to sell. | Owner, 2026-11-12: ordering only "lines" feels monotonous. Also means a connected town keeps producing work and money. |
| D23 | Time | **Time is lived, not spent.** A day is a fixed length of real time (8 minutes, a dial) and nothing but sleep moves the clock. Work is done by holding E on the thing, for as long as the step takes; there are no clock jumps. So a day holds only so many jobs. Bodging is instant, looks crooked, and several bodges on one line surface sooner. (Replaces the old `SpendHours` jumps; amends D10.) | Owner, 2026-11-13: "make days timed and you can only do so many jobs in each day, but make it take longer for the player, not accelerate more time of day per task… in-world interactions able to show mistakes that compound." |
| D24 | Who is home | **People keep hours.** Families, students and the curious are out nine to five on weekdays; everyone is asleep from ten; Sol keeps shop hours and shuts on Sundays. A lit window or a car on the drive shows who is in. A new player's first hour is exempt. **Direction:** named characters who really walk about the planet and have to be found and talked to. | Owner, 2026-11-13. Replaces the "nobody home" dice roll. |
| D25 | Staff and tickets | **Every job is a paper ticket; staff only work on tickets they are handed.** Tickets hang on a rail in your room. You take one and give it to the person, in person, at your door. No ticket, no work, and they are **paid by the job** on Friday. Leasing a depot (1 Main Street) changes that: staff collect their own tickets there each morning and go on a **weekly wage**. Each person has a narrow job (Jory: installs). Pay day, rent day and days off are on a wall calendar. Training people in new kinds of ticket by doing jobs alongside them is the next step. | Owner, 2026-11-13: "delegate in-game, directly to the staff member, until we buy a shop for them to work at; then they have to turn up to get paid… less variety in their work. Tickets are generated and we give them to the staff." |
| D26 | Panels become objects | **Agreed: every on-screen panel becomes an object you handle, starting with a real switchboard** (`DESK_AND_DAY_DESIGN.md`). The text strip goes once the objects exist; **highlights on the object and PIP explain the task as you do it.** | Owner, 2026-11-13. |
| D27 | The planet, later | **Direction, not yet scheduled:** a bigger or more detailed planet with places put down by hand rather than generated. | Owner, 2026-11-13. Revisit D12/D13 when the art track reaches buildings. |
