## Name

**TELO: Network Empire**

## Short description (shown beside the main picture; 300 characters at most, this is 284)

> Wire your first phone line by hand in 1983, from a rented room with a plywood switchboard. Knock on doors, climb poles and hire help while the job itself changes, from screwdrivers to terminals to a humming data centre, on one small planet you can drive round in a quarter of an hour.

## About this game

> **Start with a screwdriver. End with a data centre.**
>
> It's 1983. You've rented a room above a shop on Main Street, screwed a sheet of plywood to the wall, and you have one customer, if you can get their line working. TELO is a first-person job sim and business game about building a phone network from nothing, on one small planet you can walk, drive and sail around.
>
> **The job changes as you play.** At first you do everything by hand: knock on doors, carry the ladder from the van, fit the socket, run the wire, and plug your customer into the switchboard. Then you hire Jory. Then the work arrives as tickets, then on a terminal, then as lamps on a rack in a data centre you built yourself.
>
> **Do it by hand**
> - A real switchboard on your wall: jumper wires, smudged name tags, a big TEST switch.
> - Installs at real front doors, poles planted one by one across the fields, odd jobs for the neighbours.
> - Bodge any step if you're in a hurry. It's quicker today and it'll come back to you.
>
> **Hand it over**
> - Hire staff and hand them tickets in person. They're right most of the time. The rest turns up weeks later as a fault at a house you've forgotten.
> - The evening rush: more callers than lines, engaged tones, and a rack of line cards to buy your way out.
> - Every improvement creates a new problem.
>
> **One small planet**
> - A town, its neighbour Eastgate, three villages, a market town on the headland and an island across the water: one world, on foot, by van or by ferry.
> - Every customer is a person with a name, a front door, working hours and opinions.
> - Somewhere out there is NezTel, the national phone monopoly. Motto: "Nez Ensures Zero-downtime."
>
> **In early access:** Acts 0 to 5, about 20 to 30 hours, from the first wire to lighting up the data centre that brings the whole network home.

## Early access questions

**Why early access?**
> The whole of early access plays from start to finish, and the planet keeps going after it: mobile masts, then much more. I'd like players' help with the pace and the money while the rest is built, because a game about a network should be shaped by the people on it.

**About how long will it be in early access?**
> About [12–18] months. *(Owner: pick a number you're comfortable with. Players forgive slow; they don't forgive silence.)*

**How will the full version differ?**
> More of the planet and its history (mobile masts and beyond), more staff to hand work to, more variety in every job, real models where some buildings are still simple shapes, translations, and controller support for Steam Deck.

**What is the state of the early-access version?**
> Acts 0 to 5 are complete and play start to finish (20 to 30 hours). Keyboard and mouse, English only. Some buildings and rooms use simple placeholder shapes. Saves carry over between updates.

**Will the price change?**
> Yes: it will go up at 1.0. Buying in early access is the cheapest way in.

**How will you involve the community?**
> Through the Steam forums, a regular update every month or so, and a monthly letter on Ko-fi. Feedback on pace, money and what's confusing goes straight into the next update.
